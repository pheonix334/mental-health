    How this mental health survey works

    This tool uses two of the most widely trusted mental health screening questionnaires in clinical use: the PHQ-9 for depression and the GAD-7 for anxiety. Both were developed and validated through peer-reviewed research and are commonly used by primary care doctors, therapists, and psychiatrists as a first step — not a final answer — in understanding someone's symptoms.

    You'll answer 16 questions one at a time, rating how often each symptom has bothered you over the past two weeks. At the end, you'll get a combined status report: your PHQ-9 and GAD-7 scores, a severity band for each, a breakdown of which symptom areas scored highest, and general suggestions for what to consider next.

    If any answer suggests thoughts of self-harm, a crisis-resources panel appears immediately — before you even finish the survey — because that matters more than a final score.

    Severity band reference

    Minimal (0–4)
    Few or no symptoms. Continue monitoring how you feel.
    Mild (5–9)
    Some symptoms present. Self-care and monitoring are often enough, but keep an eye on changes.
    Moderate (10–14)
    Symptoms are noticeable and may be affecting daily life. Worth discussing with a doctor or therapist.
    Moderately Severe (15–19, PHQ-9 only)
    Significant symptoms. Professional support is recommended soon.
    Severe (20–27 PHQ-9 · 15–21 GAD-7)
    Substantial symptoms. Reaching out to a mental health professional is strongly recommended.

    Frequently asked questions

    The PHQ-9 (Patient Health Questionnaire-9) is a validated 9-question screening tool for depression, and the GAD-7 (Generalized Anxiety Disorder-7) is a validated 7-question screening tool for anxiety. Both are widely used by doctors and therapists worldwide as a quick, evidence-based way to gauge symptom severity over the past two weeks.
    No. This is a self-report screening tool, not a diagnosis. Only a qualified mental health professional can diagnose depression, anxiety, or any other condition, using a full clinical evaluation. This survey can help you understand your symptoms and decide whether it's worth talking to someone.
    The PHQ-9 and GAD-7 questionnaires themselves are extensively validated in clinical research and are the same tools many doctors use in-office. Their accuracy depends on answering honestly about the past two weeks. As with any self-report tool, results are a screening signal, not a clinical diagnosis.
    Each questionnaire is scored 0 to 3 per question. PHQ-9 totals of 0-4 suggest minimal depression symptoms, 5-9 mild, 10-14 moderate, 15-19 moderately severe, and 20-27 severe. GAD-7 totals of 0-4 suggest minimal anxiety symptoms, 5-9 mild, 10-14 moderate, and 15-21 severe.
    Yes, it's private. There's no account, login, or server database. Your answers are scored in your browser, and if you choose to save a result for your own tracking, it's stored privately on your own device only — never sent to us or anyone else.

    Not a diagnostic tool: This survey provides general screening information only, based on the standard PHQ-9 and GAD-7 questionnaires.
