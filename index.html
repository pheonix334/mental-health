    Why lifestyle factors matter

    Sperm production is a continuous, roughly 74-day process, plus another 1–2 weeks for maturation and transport. That means sperm quality at any given moment reflects your body's conditions over the past ~2–3 months — not just how you feel today. It also means changes you make now (quitting smoking, reducing heat exposure, improving sleep) typically take about that long to show up in actual sperm quality.

    This checklist is built from well-documented risk factors in reproductive health research — things like smoking, heat exposure, obesity, poor diet, chronic stress, and certain environmental exposures. It gives you a general sense of which areas might be worth addressing, and lets you track how your profile changes as you make adjustments.

    Category reference

    Mostly favorable
    Few or no flagged factors. Keep monitoring and maintaining current habits.
    A few areas to address
    Some factors present. Small, sustained changes in the flagged categories may help.
    Several risk factors present
    Worth actively working on the flagged categories, and considering a conversation with a doctor if trying to conceive.
    Significant risk factors
    A meaningful number of factors are present. A urologist or fertility specialist can give you an actual semen analysis and personalized guidance.
